# CRO / Commerce Plan

## Current business signals from the prior project audit

Approximately the last-month snapshot used during planning showed:

- sessions: 3,824
- cart additions: 140 (~3.66%)
- reached checkout: 104 (~2.72%)
- completed checkout: 29 (~0.76%)
- mobile web: ~77.6% of sessions
- AOV: ~CAD $106.97
- free shipping threshold: CAD $120

Treat these as historical planning inputs, not live analytics. Re-query before making current-performance claims.

## Conversion priorities

### 1. Mobile first

The majority of sessions were mobile. Improve:

- header height;
- first-view product identity;
- tap target size;
- sticky purchase affordance when helpful;
- filter UX;
- cart clarity;
- page speed;
- reduced repetition.

### 2. Make scent discovery easy

Many shoppers do not know fragrance terminology. Offer both:

- expert path: notes/family/format filters;
- novice path: mood, occasion, intensity, familiar descriptors.

### 3. Sampling funnel

Make sampling a first-class purchase path:

> Unsure? Sample → discover → return for full size.

A future full-size credit program is a business decision, not something the theme should invent.

### 4. Product cards

Must always show price and clear identity. Do not hide essential information behind hover.

### 5. PDP above-fold

Show:
- title/scent;
- rating;
- notes/descriptor;
- format/size;
- price;
- availability;
- ATC;
- sample CTA;
- shipping threshold.

### 6. Trust

Preserve Judge.me. Place reviews near the buying decision, not only at the bottom.

Use factual shipping/returns language rather than generic trust badges.

### 7. Cart/AOV

The historical AOV was roughly $13 below the $120 free-shipping threshold, making a tasteful progress indicator potentially useful.

Use:
- free-shipping progress;
- one relevant sample/cross-sell;
- clear subtotal;
- accelerated checkout.

Avoid forcing unrelated products.

## Fragrance UX research distilled from prior research

Useful competitive patterns observed during the research phase:

- Discovery sets reduce commitment risk.
- “Try before committing” models work because fragrance is hard to buy digitally.
- Fragrance finders work best when they ask about mood/occasion/personality, not only note literacy.
- Product-list duplication by every variant can overwhelm users, especially on mobile.
- Active filter chips are clearer than only showing a filter count.

These patterns are inspiration, not requirements to copy literally.
