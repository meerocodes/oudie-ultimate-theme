A wedding fragrance bar gives guests a guided way to discover scents during your celebration. Before booking one in Toronto, Mississauga or the GTA, ask how the experience will fit your venue, reception schedule and guest count. Clear details make it easier to assess a proposal.

1. What will guests actually do?

Ask the provider to describe the guest journey from arrival to finish. Will guests explore a curated selection, receive recommendations or take part in a hands-on activity? A fragrance bar and a blending workshop are different formats, so clarify which one you are discussing.

OUDIE offers guided fragrance bars and separate private workshops. Start with the experience you want guests to have, then discuss how it can fit your wedding.

2. When will the bar be part of the celebration?

Consider the welcome period, reception transitions and the evening schedule. Share your planned timing with the provider and your planner. Ask how arrivals will be handled and whether the proposal suits the way guests will move through the event.

Do not assume that an activity can run for the entire reception. Confirm the service window and any setup or pack-down arrangements in writing.

3. What does the venue need to provide?

Ask about the footprint, table requirements, access and setup location. Your venue coordinator can help review the space and any restrictions. If fragrance will be used indoors, discuss the placement thoughtfully alongside dining, flowers and the other parts of your celebration.

Share the venue name as soon as it is available. If you are still choosing, provide the city and the type of setting so the first conversation can begin.

4. How is the scent selection chosen?

Tell the provider what you and your partner enjoy. Ask how a menu is curated and whether there are presentation options to discuss. You can explore OUDIE’s scent catalogue beforehand, while keeping in mind that an event’s selection is agreed separately.

You do not need to pick every scent before making an inquiry. A short description of the atmosphere you want is a helpful starting point.

5. What is included in the proposal?

Confirm staffing, materials, any guest takeaway, presentation and the service window. If bottles or personalisation are part of your request, ask what is possible and how those details affect the proposal. Do not assume a specific bottle size, quantity or inclusion until it is confirmed.

Ask about the process for changes to attendance, travel arrangements and booking terms. A useful proposal should let you understand the scope as well as the price.

6. What should you send in the first inquiry?

  • Your names and contact email.
  • The wedding date, or an approximate period if undecided.
  • The city and venue if known.
  • An approximate guest count.
  • The fragrance experience you have in mind.
  • Questions about timing, presentation or inclusions.
