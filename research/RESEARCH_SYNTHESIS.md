# Research Synthesis

This file distills the prior comparative research used to shape the redesign. It is design/CRO inspiration, not a source of facts about OUDIE itself.

## Fragrance ecommerce patterns

### Sampling

Brands such as Byredo and Diptyque use discovery sets to reduce commitment risk. Aesop has used “try first” concepts around matching samples/full products. The strategic takeaway for OUDIE is to make sampling prominent, not to copy another brand's exact commercial policy.

### Scent finder

Jo Malone-style scent finders ask about recipient, occasion, mood/personality and desired feeling instead of requiring note expertise. For OUDIE, discovery should support both novice language and expert note filtering.

### Collection UX

UX research used during planning highlighted that duplicating every variant as a separate product-list card can create clutter, especially on mobile. Applied filter chips are more understandable than a generic “3 filters” count.

### PDP

Strong ecommerce PDPs keep price, options, availability, reviews, purchase CTA and fulfillment/trust information visible near the purchase decision. OUDIE should not bury sample discovery and scent notes below several repeated blocks.

## Fragrance experience research

Fragrance-bar/workshop competitors reviewed during planning typically explain:

- what guests do;
- available scent choices;
- guided facilitation;
- take-home fragrance;
- custom branding/labels when offered;
- duration;
- group fit;
- event setup/travel.

The design takeaway: an Experiences page must answer operational questions, not only look beautiful.

## OUDIE theme comparison distilled

### Live site strengths

- strong scent intelligence;
- useful Explore Our Scents experience;
- SEO/local content depth;
- functional PDP;
- Judge.me/social proof;
- efficient mobile header.

### Live site weaknesses

- feels like a base theme with OUDIE customization layered on;
- product cards/collections weaker than scent intelligence;
- broad collection can mix unrelated product types;
- luxury/distinctiveness underdeveloped;
- repeated/legacy content patterns.

### Previous imported redesign strengths

- stronger editorial/luxury direction;
- improved collection framing;
- stronger storytelling and cart/AOV ideas;
- more distinctive homepage.

### Previous imported redesign weaknesses

- mobile execution felt heavy/broken;
- inherited assumptions and code complexity;
- some hardcoded claims/copy;
- product pricing and filter-state issues;
- repetition on PDP;
- did not feel sufficiently robust against real store data.

## Final research conclusion

The new scratch theme should combine:

**live site's intelligence and trust** + **the redesign's editorial ambition** + **a cleaner, data-first, mobile-first implementation**.
